# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food. They use sunlight, carbon dioxide and water, and produce glucose and oxygen. The overall reaction is: carbon dioxide + water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, gives glucose + oxygen.

Chlorophyll is the green pigment that absorbs sunlight. It is found inside chloroplasts, which are the cell organelles where photosynthesis takes place. Chlorophyll is what makes leaves look green.

Carbon dioxide enters the leaf through tiny pores called stomata, mostly present on the underside of the leaf. Guard cells open and close the stomata. Oxygen and water vapour leave the leaf through the same pores.

Water and minerals are absorbed by the roots from the soil and carried to the leaves through the xylem. The glucose made in the leaves is stored as starch or transported to other parts of the plant.

Photosynthesis is important because it is the source of food for almost all living things and it releases the oxygen that we breathe.
